# sourced
1 | John, exiled on Patmos, sees the risen Christ among seven lampstands, with eyes like fire and a face like the sun: “Don’t be afraid. I am the first and the last, and the Living one.” || “Revelation” is the Greek apokalupsis, an unveiling: God shares his message through symbols, numbers and word pictures, and Jesus Christ is both its source and its subject [[tosn:Revelation 1:1]]. Having defeated death, the Living One holds “the keys of Death and of Hades” [[tosn:Revelation 1:18]] [[bible:Revelation 1:18]].
2 | Letters to the churches of Ephesus (you have left your first love), Smyrna (be faithful to death), Pergamum and Thyatira. || Each letter opens with the same command, showing the weight of the message [[tosn:Revelation 2:1]]. Ephesus had lost its first love for Christ and for one another [[tosn:Revelation 2:4]]. Smyrna is the only one of the seven cities still standing, as Izmir in modern Turkey [[tosn:Revelation 2:8]].
3 | Letters to Sardis, Philadelphia and lukewarm Laodicea: “Behold, I stand at the door and knock.” || Nearby Hierapolis was famous for healing hot springs and Colossae for cold, refreshing ones; Laodicea’s water was smelly and lukewarm [[tosn:Revelation 3:15-16]]. The city was proud of its independence and rebuilt itself after an earthquake in AD 60 [[tosn:Revelation 3:14]].
4 | A door opens in heaven: John sees God’s throne with a rainbow, twenty-four elders and four living creatures singing, “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God, the Almighty.” || The throne room continues the visions of Isaiah 6:1–4, Ezekiel 1:4–28 and Daniel 7:9–10 [[tosn:Revelation 4:1-11]] [[bsn:Revelation 4:1-11]]. Before any judgement falls, God is seen ruling on his throne.
5 | A scroll sealed with seven seals: no one can open it until the Lion of Judah appears, and he is a Lamb that had been slain. All creation sings, “Worthy is the Lamb.” || John hears of a Lion and sees “a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain” [[bible:Revelation 5:6]]: once slaughtered, now standing, the Lamb pictures Jesus’ death and resurrection [[tosn:Revelation 5:6]]. He wins through love that sacrifices everything [[bsn:Revelation 5:1-14]].
6 | The Lamb opens six seals: four horsemen (conquest, war, famine, death), the martyrs’ cry “How long?”, and a great earthquake. || The four horses and riders draw on Zechariah 1:8–11 and 6:1–8 [[tosn:Revelation 6:1-8]]. Sword, famine, disease and wild animals (compare Ezekiel 14:21) sum up the hardships of life on earth [[tosn:Revelation 6:7-8]].
7 | 144,000 from the tribes of Israel are sealed, then John sees a great multitude no one can count, from every nation, before the throne. God will wipe away every tear. || John hears the number of those sealed, then sees a great multitude [[bsn:Revelation 7:1-17]]. God’s seal marks people as his own, owned and protected (Ephesians 1:13–14) [[tosn:Revelation 7:1-3]].
8 | The seventh seal: silence in heaven, prayers rising like incense, then seven trumpets begin with plagues on the earth. || The trumpet judgements are like the plagues of Egypt (Exodus 7–11) [[tosn:Revelation 8:2-11:19]], but they reach far beyond one country [[bsn:Revelation 8:6-13]].
9 | The fifth and sixth trumpets: locusts from the abyss and a vast army; yet people do not repent. || These judgements should lead people to repent, but they do not [[tosn:Revelation 9:1-21]]. Those with God’s seal are spared [[tosn:Revelation 9:3-4]].
10 | A mighty angel with a little scroll; John eats it, sweet in his mouth but bitter in his stomach. || As with Ezekiel, the scroll tastes sweet (Ezekiel 3:1–3) [[tosn:Revelation 10:8-10]] [[bsn:Revelation 10:1-11]]. Unlike Ezekiel, John must prophesy about all the peoples of the world [[tosn:Revelation 10:11]].
11 | Two witnesses prophesy, are killed, and raised. The seventh trumpet: “The kingdom of the world has become the Kingdom of our Lord, and of his Christ. He will reign forever and ever.” || The seventh trumpet brings a view of God’s eternal kingdom, as the first series of judgements did [[tosn:Revelation 8:2-11:19]]: “He will reign forever and ever!” [[bible:Revelation 11:15]]
12 | A woman clothed with the sun gives birth to a son; a great dragon tries to devour him. Michael defeats the dragon, Satan, who is thrown down. Believers overcome “because of the Lamb’s blood”. || The woman stands for God’s people, who give birth to the Messiah [[tosn:Revelation 12:1-14:20]]; the scene sums up Jesus’ life from birth to resurrection [[tosn:Revelation 12:5]]. The battle goes back to Genesis 3:14–15, and Herod’s attempt to kill the child was Satan’s work [[tosn:Revelation 12:3-4]].
13 | A beast rises from the sea, given authority by the dragon, and a second beast from the land makes people worship it. Its number is 666. || In Hebrew and Greek, letters also stand for numbers [[tosn:Revelation 13:16-17]]. John may have reached 666 from a Hebrew spelling of Caesar Neron; later Greek-speaking scribes changed it to 616 in some manuscripts, likely to match the Greek [[tosn:Revelation 13:18]]. The beast likely represents Roman power [[tosn:Revelation 13:1-10]].
14 | The Lamb stands on Mount Zion with the 144,000. Angels proclaim the eternal gospel and the fall of Babylon; a harvest of the earth. || “Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord” [[bible:Revelation 14:13]]: God wants those who suffer persecution to share his rest [[tosn:Revelation 14:13]], blessed even if they are put to death [[bsn:Revelation 14:6-13]].
15 | The victors sing the song of Moses and the Lamb; seven angels receive bowls of God’s wrath. || Their song is like Moses’ song in Exodus 15 [[bsn:Revelation 15:1-8]]. The song of Moses and the Lamb shows God’s purposes in the old and new covenants joined together [[tosn:Revelation 15:3-4]].
16 | The seven bowls are poured out: sores, blood, scorching heat, darkness; the kings gather at the place called in Hebrew “Megiddo”. || Many Bibles read “Armageddon” (Harmagedon), likely from the Hebrew har, “mountain”, and Megiddo. The fortress guarded the main pass through the Carmel range, where many armies fought, and the name became a symbol of the final conflict between God and evil [[tosn:Revelation 16:16]].
17 | A woman, “Babylon the great”, rides a scarlet beast, drunk with the blood of the saints. The Lamb will overcome, “for he is Lord of lords, and King of kings.” || The seven heads stand for the seven hills on which Rome was built [[tosn:Revelation 17:9]]; in symbolic language John calls Rome Babylon, which stood for the world’s idolatry [[tosn:Revelation 17:5]].
18 | Babylon falls; kings and merchants lament her lost luxury. “Come out of her, my people.” || “Fallen, fallen is Babylon” echoes Isaiah 21:9 [[tosn:Revelation 18:2]], and the laments recall Ezekiel 27 over Tyre [[tosn:Revelation 18:9-19]].
19 | Heaven sings “Hallelujah!” for the marriage supper of the Lamb. Christ rides a white horse, called Faithful and True and the Word of God, and defeats the beast. || Hallelujah is Hebrew for “praise the Lord” [[bsn:Revelation 19:1-10]]; in this Bible’s New Testament it is found only in this chapter [[bible:Revelation 19:1-6]]. The final Hallelujah proclaims that God reigns as the Almighty [[tosn:Revelation 19:6-8]].
20 | Satan is bound for a thousand years; the martyrs reign with Christ. Satan is released, defeated, and the dead are judged before a great white throne. || Christians read the thousand years in three main ways: amillennial (no literal thousand-year reign), postmillennial (Christ returns after it) and premillennial (Christ returns before it begins) [[tbd:013:Millennium]].
21 | “I saw a new heaven and a new earth.” The holy city, new Jerusalem, comes down from God: “God himself will be with them… he will wipe away every tear… death will be no more.” “Behold, I am making all things new.” || “Behold, God’s dwelling is with people” [[bible:Revelation 21:3]]. The city is a perfect cube, 12,000 stadia each way, a number standing for God’s people [[tosn:Revelation 21:16]]. There is no temple, because the whole city is holy [[bsn:Revelation 21:22-22:5]].
22 | The river of the water of life flows from the throne, and the tree of life bears fruit for the healing of the nations. “Behold, I come quickly.” “Come, Lord Jesus.” || After humans sinned they lost the tree of life (Genesis 3:22–24); now it is freely open [[tosn:Revelation 22:2]]. The river is like the one Ezekiel saw [[bsn:Revelation 21:22-22:5]]. The Bible closes with a prayer: “Amen! Yes, come, Lord Jesus” [[bible:Revelation 22:20]]; early Christians sometimes prayed “Come, Lord” [[tosn:1 Corinthians 16:22]].
