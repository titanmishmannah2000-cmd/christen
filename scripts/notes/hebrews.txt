1 | God spoke in many ways through the prophets, but now through his Son, the radiance of his glory, who made purification for sins and sat down at God’s right hand. The Son is greater than the angels. || The writer gathers Old Testament passages to show that Jesus is greater than the angels, as Jewish and Christian teachers often linked passages together [[tosn:Hebrews 1:5-14]]. The words translated “radiance” and “very image” appear nowhere else in the New Testament [[tosn:Hebrews 1:3]].
2 | Don’t drift away. Jesus was made a little lower than the angels and shared our flesh and blood, to destroy the power of death and help those who are tempted. || Psalm 8:4–6 moves the argument from Christ’s divine nature to his human nature [[tosn:Hebrews 2:5-9]]. 2:12 quotes Psalm 22:22, from the psalm of Christ’s suffering [[tosn:Hebrews 2:12]].
3 | Jesus is greater than Moses: a son over God’s house, not a servant in it. “Today if you will hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts.” || The writer urges his readers with Psalm 95: “Today if you will hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts” [[tosn:Hebrews 3:15]]. Israel rebelled in the wilderness and did not enter God’s rest [[tosn:Hebrews 3:16-19]].
4 | A Sabbath rest remains for God’s people. “The word of God is living and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword.” We have a great high priest who sympathizes with our weaknesses, so approach the throne of grace boldly. || Jesus was “in all points tempted like we are, yet without sin” [[bible:Hebrews 4:15]]. Unlike earthly high priests, he did not need to offer sacrifices for his own sins [[tosn:Hebrews 4:15]].
5 | Every high priest is chosen from among men; Christ was appointed by God, “a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek”. He learned obedience through suffering. || The main text is Psalm 110:4, where God promises that the Son will be a priest for ever like Melchizedek [[tosn:Hebrews 5:1-10]].
6 | Move on to maturity. A severe warning about falling away, then encouragement: God’s promise and oath are an anchor for the soul. || This is one of the hardest passages in the New Testament. It warns about those who abandon the Christian faith, like the Israelites who fell in the wilderness [[tosn:Hebrews 6:4-8]]; yet the writer is confident his hearers truly know God [[tosn:Hebrews 6:9]].
7 | Melchizedek, king of righteousness and peace, was greater than Abraham. Jesus is a priest forever, who “he lives forever to make intercession” for us. || The writer reads Genesis 14:17–20 together with Psalm 110:4 to show that Melchizedek’s priesthood is greater than the Levites’ [[tosn:Hebrews 7:1-10]] [[tosn:Hebrews 7:3]].
8 | Jesus serves in the true tabernacle in heaven and mediates a better covenant. Jeremiah’s new covenant is quoted in full. || The writer quotes Jeremiah 31:31–34, God’s promise of a new covenant [[tosn:Hebrews 8:7-13]].
9 | The earthly tabernacle and the Day of Atonement were a shadow. Christ entered heaven itself “through his own blood… having obtained eternal redemption.” || “Apart from shedding of blood there is no remission” [[bible:Hebrews 9:22]]: blood was used for cleansing and atonement under the law (Leviticus 16) [[tosn:Hebrews 9:22]]. Christ offered his own blood, once for all [[tosn:Hebrews 9:11-10:18]].
10 | The law’s sacrifices could never take away sins, but Christ offered one sacrifice for all time. “let’s draw near… let’s hold fast… Let’s consider how to provoke one another to love and good works,” not forsaking meeting together. || 10:5–7 quotes Psalm 40:6–8. God’s plan was for Christ to die for sins as a sacrifice that needed to happen only once [[tosn:Hebrews 10:5-7]] [[tosn:Hebrews 10:8-10]]. Then he sat down at God’s right hand (Psalm 110:1) [[tosn:Hebrews 10:12-13]].
11 | “Faith is assurance of things hoped for, proof of things not seen.” The great roll call of faith: Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Moses, Rahab and many more. || “Faith is assurance of things hoped for, proof of things not seen” [[bible:Hebrews 11:1]]. Under the new covenant God’s people live as they did under the old: trusting his promises and enduring hardship [[tosn:Hebrews 11:39-40]].
12 | “let’s run with perseverance the race that is set before us, looking to Jesus, the author and perfecter of faith” God disciplines his children. You have come to Mount Zion, the city of the living God. || Mount Sinai stands for the old covenant and Mount Zion for the new [[tosn:Hebrews 12:18-24]] [[bsn:Hebrews 12:18-29]].
13 | Keep loving one another; show hospitality, “for in doing so, some have entertained angels without knowing it.” “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever.” || The closing blessing gathers the letter’s main themes. Calling Jesus “the great shepherd of the sheep” recalls Psalm 23 [[tosn:Hebrews 13:20-21]] [[bible:Hebrews 13:20]] and John 10 [[bsn:Hebrews 13:20-25]].
