# sourced
1 | “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… The Word became flesh, and lived among us.” John the Baptist points to Jesus: “Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!” The first disciples follow. || “In the beginning” repeats the opening words of Genesis: Jesus is the one through whom God made everything [[tosn:John 1:1]]. As Jacob saw a ladder to heaven at Bethel, Nathanael will see God at work in Jesus [[tosn:John 1:51]] [[bible:John 1:51]].
2 | At a wedding in Cana Jesus turns water into wine, his first sign. In Jerusalem he clears the temple: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” || John places the clearing of the temple early in Jesus’ ministry; Matthew, Mark and Luke place it near the end [[tosn:Mark 11:12-25]]. The temple Jesus spoke of was his own body, the true place where God is present [[tosn:John 2:19-21]]. John mentions three Passovers, so many scholars think Jesus’ public ministry lasted about three years [[tosn:John 2:13]].
3 | Nicodemus visits Jesus at night and is told he must be born again (or “from above”). “For God so loved the world, that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life.” || Nicodemus takes “born again” to mean a physical birth; Jesus means a new birth by God’s Spirit [[tosn:John 3:3]] [[bsn:John 3:1-21]]. In Greek the word for wind and for spirit is the same, pneuma [[tosn:John 3:8]]. Eternal life is God’s gift, and it begins now [[tosn:John 3:36]].
4 | At Jacob’s well Jesus asks a Samaritan woman for water and offers her “living water”. She brings her town to him. He heals an official’s son. || Jesus taught that all people could worship God, with the Spirit’s help, and not only in one place [[bsn:John 4:1-26]]: “God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth” [[bible:John 4:24]] [[tosn:John 4:24]].
5 | Jesus heals a man paralysed for thirty-eight years at the pool of Bethesda, on the Sabbath. He says he does what he sees the Father doing. || Bethesda was a famous place of healing [[bsn:John 5:1-15]]. The chapter unfolds like a courtroom trial, with Jesus making his defence and then bringing his own charges [[tosn:John 5:1-40]] [[tosn:John 5:41-47]].
6 | Jesus feeds five thousand and walks on the sea. He says, “I am the bread of life.” Many disciples leave, but Peter says, “Lord, to whom would we go? You have the words of eternal life.” || The feeding recalls the manna in the wilderness, and some expected the Messiah to give bread from heaven again [[tosn:John 6:1-15]]. “I am the bread of life” is the first of Jesus’ “I am” sayings, which recall God’s name in Exodus 3:14 [[tosn:John 6:35]]. His words point ahead to the Lord’s Supper [[tosn:John 6:53-58]].
7 | At the Feast of Tabernacles Jesus teaches in the temple: “If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink.” People argue whether he is the Christ. || At the Feast of Tabernacles water was poured on the altar each day with prayers for rain [[tosn:John 7:37-38]]. On the last day Jesus says that the water of new life comes from him [[bsn:John 7:37-53]].
8 | A woman caught in adultery: “He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone.” Jesus says, “I am the light of the world,” and “Before Abraham came into existence, I AM.” || The story of the woman caught in adultery (7:53–8:11) is not in the earliest Greek manuscripts and was probably added later, but it is likely a true story from the life of Jesus [[tosn:John 7:53-8:11]]. “Before Abraham came into existence, I AM” recalls the name God revealed to Moses (Exodus 3:14) [[tosn:John 8:58]] [[bible:John 8:58]].
9 | Jesus heals a man born blind, who is questioned and thrown out of the synagogue. “I was blind, now I see.” || The story ends with a reversal: the man born blind comes to see, while those who claim to see are blind [[tosn:John 9:1-41]] [[tosn:John 9:40-41]].
10 | “I am the door.” “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” “I and the Father are one.” || Israel’s leaders were meant to shepherd God’s flock as God does (Ezekiel 34) [[tosn:John 10:11-14]]; the good shepherd stands between his sheep and danger [[tosn:John 10:11-13]]. “I have other sheep, which are not of this fold” [[bible:John 10:16]].
11 | Lazarus dies. Jesus tells Martha, “I am the resurrection and the life.” Jesus weeps, then calls Lazarus out of the tomb. The leaders plot to kill Jesus. || Raising Lazarus is the seventh sign in John’s Gospel [[bsn:John 11:1-16]], and “I am the resurrection and the life” promises victory over death [[tosn:John 11:25]]. “Jesus wept” [[bible:John 11:35]]. Caiaphas spoke for political reasons, but his words were truer than he knew: Jesus would die for the nation [[tosn:John 11:50-51]] [[bsn:John 11:46-57]].
12 | Mary anoints Jesus’s feet. Jesus enters Jerusalem to cries of Hosanna. Greeks ask to see him. “Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains by itself alone.” || Until now Jesus had said his hour had not yet come; the arrival of the Greeks marks its beginning [[tosn:John 12:23]]. He rides a donkey, not coming as a warrior to conquer nations [[tosn:John 12:14]].
13 | At the last supper Jesus washes his disciples’ feet. Judas leaves into the night. “A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another… as I have loved you.” || At the last Passover meal Jesus washes his disciples’ feet [[tosn:John 13:1-38]], an example of the humble service he calls them to [[tosn:John 13:14-15]]. Here his farewell words begin, often called the Upper Room Discourse (13:31–17:26) [[tosn:John 13:31-17:26]].
14 | “Don’t let your heart be troubled… In my Father’s house are many homes.” “I am the way, the truth, and the life.” Jesus promises the Holy Spirit, the Counsellor, and gives his peace. || Jesus comforts his disciples before he leaves [[bsn:John 14:1-21]]. In his Father’s house are many rooms [[tosn:John 14:2]]. The Spirit is “another Counselor”, who carries on Jesus’ work by advising, defending and protecting believers [[bible:John 14:16]] [[tosn:John 14:16]].
15 | “I am the true vine… you are the branches.” Remain in my love. “Greater love has no one than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends.” || The vine has long pictured God’s people (Psalm 80; Isaiah 5) [[tosn:John 15:1]]. Fruit comes from staying joined to the vine [[tosn:John 15:2-3]], and Jesus calls his disciples friends, as God called Abraham and Moses [[tosn:John 15:14-16]].
16 | The Spirit will convict the world and guide the disciples into all truth. Their grief will turn to joy. “In the world you have trouble; but cheer up! I have overcome the world.” || The cross would bring the disciples weeping, but their sorrow would turn to joy when Jesus rose [[tosn:John 16:20]], like a woman whose pain in labour gives way to joy [[bible:John 16:21]].
17 | Jesus prays for himself, for his disciples, and for all who will believe through them, “that they may all be one”. “This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God.” || This is Jesus’ longest prayer, often called the “high priestly prayer” [[tosn:John 17:1-26]]. He prays that all his followers may be one, so that the world may believe [[bible:John 17:21]] [[tosn:John 17:23]].
18 | Jesus is arrested in a garden, Peter cuts off a servant’s ear, Jesus is questioned by Annas and Caiaphas, Peter denies him, and Pilate asks, “What is truth?” || Even the Roman soldiers fall to the ground when Jesus says “I am” [[tosn:John 18:6]]. He accepts the title of king, but his kingdom does not rival Rome [[tosn:John 18:36-37]]: “My Kingdom is not of this world” [[bible:John 18:36]].
19 | Jesus is flogged and crowned with thorns; Pilate says, “Behold, the man!” He is crucified, entrusts his mother to the beloved disciple, says “It is finished,” and dies. Blood and water flow from his side. || “It is finished”: Jesus cries out that the work he came to do is complete; on the cross he is not a victim but a servant doing God’s will [[tosn:John 19:30]]. His clothes, his unbroken bones and his pierced side all fulfil the Scriptures [[bsn:John 19:17-37]].
20 | Mary Magdalene finds the tomb empty; the risen Jesus calls her by name. He appears to the disciples, breathes the Spirit on them, and later shows Thomas his wounds: “My Lord and my God!” || Mary Magdalene is the first to see the risen Lord [[tosn:John 20:18]]. John writes so that everyone will believe that Jesus is the Messiah and the Son of God [[bsn:John 20:19-31]]: “that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name” [[bible:John 20:31]].
21 | By the Sea of Galilee the risen Jesus gives a great catch of fish and cooks breakfast. Three times he asks Peter, “Do you love me?” and says, “Feed my sheep.” || The charcoal fire recalls the night Peter denied Jesus [[tosn:John 21:9]] [[bible:John 18:18]], and three times Jesus asks Peter whether he loves him, once for each denial [[tosn:John 21:15-17]] [[bible:John 21:15-17]] The 153 fish show Jesus’ generous provision, not a hidden code [[tosn:John 21:11]].
